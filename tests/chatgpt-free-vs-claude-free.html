ChatGPT Free vs Claude Free: Same 5 Prompts, Tested (2026)

By Raghav WaliaTested on 16 November 2026, 1:05-2:20 AM IST
Home/Tests/ChatGPT Free vs Claude Free

By Raghav Walia · five prompts, ten answers, no edits, screenshots kept

Most comparisons of these two tools open with the paid plans. As a student, I wanted the boring question answered: what do the free versions actually give me, on the work I do every week?

I have used both for about eight months, mostly on the free tiers, and my guess before this test was wrong. So instead of guessing, I wrote five prompts that cover what a student actually does - an essay, a bit of code, a summary, a percentage question, and a resume line - and ran each one in both tools, in a fresh chat, with no follow-ups.

How I tested

  • Five prompts, one new chat each. No follow-up instructions, no retries, no edits to the prompt after seeing an answer.
  • Both on the free plan. ChatGPT Free and Claude Free, logged in normally, on 16 November 2026 between 1:05 and 2:20 AM IST.
  • Everything kept. The screenshots below are the answers as they arrived, not the ones I would have written.
  • Scored 1-5 on three things: was it correct, was it clear, and how much would I have to edit before using it.

Round 1: The essay

Prompt: "Write a 200-word essay on why students should learn to manage money."

ChatGPT came in at about 185 words and followed the format exactly: title, four paragraphs, conclusion. It reads like a school essay, which for this prompt is fine, but the sentences are flat and the middle two paragraphs could swap places without anyone noticing.

Claude wrote better prose - "financial literacy builds independence", the point about interest and credit cards - but it repeated the title twice at the top and ran to roughly 230 words, which breaks the one instruction I gave it. Better writing, worse obedience.

ChatGPT 4/5, Claude 4/5. Winner: ChatGPT, because the prompt had a word limit and only one of them respected it.

Round 2: The coding question

Prompt: "Write a Python function that checks whether a number is prime, and explain it in simple words."

Both functions are correct. The difference is what they hand you. ChatGPT gives the textbook version (loop from 2 to the square root), an example block, a table of inputs and outputs, and a section explaining why the square root is enough. It is thorough to the point of being long.

Claude's version is the one I would actually keep: it rejects numbers below 2, handles 2 separately, throws out every other even number early, and then steps through odd divisors only. Same output, fewer loops. Its explanation of why the square root works - "36 = 4 × 9, and 4 is below 6" - is the line a first-year student needs.

ChatGPT 4/5, Claude 5/5. Winner: Claude.

Round 3: The summary

Prompt: a 300-word paragraph on money habits, then "Summarize this in 3 bullet points."

ChatGPT gave three short bullets, each one a single idea, and the whole thing fits on a sticky note. Nothing invented, nothing left out.

Claude gave three bullets too, and they are accurate, but each one carries a colon and two clauses. They read more like three small paragraphs than three points. On a phone, at speed, ChatGPT's version is the one you can scan.

ChatGPT 5/5, Claude 4/5. Winner: ChatGPT.

Round 4: The percentage question

Prompt: "A shirt costs Rs 800 after a 20% discount. What was the original price? Show steps."

Both got Rs 1,000. The screenshots are the whole story here: ChatGPT's maths arrives as a broken formula - numbers and bars stacked on separate lines, in the browser, unreadable - and the answer is right only if you already know the method. That is a display problem more than a reasoning problem, but you cannot show that to a teacher.

Claude wrote four numbered steps, substituted 0.80 × x = 800, solved, then verified it by taking 20% of 1,000 back off. It also named the mistake most people make: adding 20% of 800 (Rs 160) to get Rs 960, which is wrong because the discount came off the original price.

ChatGPT 3/5, Claude 5/5. Winner: Claude, easily.

Round 5: The resume line

Prompt: "Write a 3-line resume summary for a first-year college student applying for an internship in marketing."

ChatGPT produced three lines you could paste into a resume today - no bracketed blanks, no editing, done. It then closed with "Do you like this personality?", which is a strange thing to ask a person applying for an internship.

Claude's version is sharper writing but full of placeholders: [Your Major], [University], [X%]. It also added three tips on how to fill them in, which is genuinely useful advice and completely useless if you wanted the finished line.

ChatGPT 4/5, Claude 4/5. Winner: ChatGPT, on the strength of being usable without edits.

Limits I noticed

I did not hit a limit in this test. Five messages each, no warning, no "you have reached your limit" screen, no slowdown on either tool at 1-2 AM IST. That is not proof that the free plans have no limits - both do, and they are counted differently - it just means five prompts is nowhere near close enough to find them.

What I did notice: neither tool remembered the earlier rounds. Each prompt went into a fresh chat, so nothing carried over, which is exactly how a student uses these tools on a phone between classes.

Verdict

If you are writing, coding or doing maths, Claude Free gave the better answers. Two of its three wins were not close: the prime function is the version that survives a code review, and the maths steps are the only ones you could hand in.

If you want a tool that does what you asked, ChatGPT Free is more obedient. Word limits, bullet counts and "3 lines" mean something to it. That matters more than prose quality when you are rushing an assignment at midnight.

For a student on zero budget, the honest answer is boring: use ChatGPT when the instructions matter, use Claude when the thinking matters, and do not pay for either until you have hit a real limit twice. Which paid plan to pick when you do is a separate question, and a dearer one - the Business vs Plus comparison is where I worked that out.

If you want the wider set, the AI writing tools guide covers the tools that are actually better than both of these at long-form work.

Questions people ask

Is ChatGPT Free enough for students?

For essays, summaries and short explanations, yes. On this test it followed instructions better than Claude and gave a usable resume line with no editing. It struggled to display maths cleanly.

Is Claude Free better for writing?

The prose is better - clearer sentences, fewer filler lines - but it went over the 200-word limit by about 30 words and repeated the title. If a word count matters, check it before you submit.

Do the free plans have limits?

They do, but I did not hit one in five messages on either tool on 16 November 2026. Both count usage differently, and the limits change often, so test your own workload rather than trusting a number from a blog.
