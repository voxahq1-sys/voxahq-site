The AI Free Tier Is Shrinking: What Five Tools Changed in 2026

19 November 2026 · 8 min read · VOXA editorial

Every AI tool starts you on a free plan and then quietly moves the line. In 2026 the line moved in one direction for most of them, and the ones that moved it wrote the change on their pricing page instead of sending an email about it.

The short answer: two of the five tools below removed their free plan completely, one removed the cheap middle plan, one cut prices by 87%, and one left its free limit exactly where it was. The lesson is not "AI is getting expensive". It is that the entry price is now a moving number, and it moves without notice.

The pattern: a free plan is marketing spend

A free tier is not charity. It is the cheapest customer acquisition channel a software company has, and it gets cut when the company stops needing it. A tool that spent 2024 giving away 200 messages a month can spend 2026 asking $150 for the first paid seat, because the users it wants are already inside.

What matters when that happens is not the headline price. It is the unit. Every one of these tools now bills on credits, requests or messages, and the number of units is where the real cost hides.

1. Chatbase: the free plan is gone entirely

Chatbase used to run a free tier and a $40 entry plan. Both are gone from the pricing page. The cheapest plan now is Basic at $150 a month billed monthly, or $120 a month if you pay for a year, and that includes 4,000 message credits. Business is $500 a month for 15,000 credits, or $400 a month on the annual price.

Where it breaks: 4,000 credits is roughly 130 short support conversations in a month. Past that you either jump to the $500 plan or buy add-on credits at $40 per 1,000. A five-page business site that wanted a free chatbot has no free door left at all.

If you are in that position, the honest alternative is not a cheaper chatbot. It is a contact form, a written FAQ and a saved reply bank. That costs $0 and answers most of the same questions.

2. Ideogram: the free column disappeared from the table

The pricing page no longer lists a free plan. The cheapest option is Plus at $20 a month or $180 billed annually, which carries 2,400 priority credits a month. Pro is $60 a month, or $504 annually, with 7,500 priority credits.

Where it breaks: the page sells credits, not images, and it does not publish a fixed images-per-credit figure. That means you cannot work out your real monthly volume until you have generated for a week. If your plan depends on a number of images, budget on credits and check what one generation costs you before you commit to a year.

3. LlamaIndex: the $50 middle step vanished

The $50 Starter plan is no longer offered. What replaced it is a pay-as-you-go entry: 10,000 free credits every month, then $1.50 per 1,000 credits. The next step up is Pro at $500 a month, which includes 400,000 credits, with a $1.25 per 1,000 rate past that.

Where it breaks: 10,000 credits covers roughly 10,000 plain pages in a month. A PDF full of tables does not count as one page per page, it can eat several credits each, so a 300 page price list can cost far more than 300 credits.

This is the one change that actually helps a small user. Paying $1.50 only when you go over is better than a $50 floor you may not use, as long as you keep an eye on the credit burn.

4. Exa: the one that went the other way

Exa cut monitoring from $15 per 1,000 requests to $2 per 1,000, a drop of about 87%. Search starts at $4 per 1,000 requests on the entry tier. For anyone running a price or news watch, that is a four-figure annual difference at volume.

Where it breaks: zero data retention is no longer a checkbox on the cheap plan. It sits behind the Enterprise tier, which is priced by conversation. Cheaper monitoring and stricter data terms arrived in the same release.

5. QuillBot: the free limit that did not move

Not every free tier shrank. QuillBot's free plan still states 125 words per paraphrase, two paraphrasing modes, limited humanising and a limited AI detector. That number has been quoted across the web as cut to 100 words, and the vendor page says otherwise.

Which is the whole point of this post. Second-hand pricing lists copy each other, including the wrong versions. If a price matters to your monthly bill, read the page it comes from, not the summary of it.

The five changes, side by side

  • Chatbase - free plan removed. First paid tier is Basic at $150 a month monthly, $120 a month yearly, with 4,000 message credits.
  • Ideogram - free plan removed from the pricing table. Plus at $20 a month or $180 a year, 2,400 priority credits. Pro at $60 a month.
  • LlamaIndex - the $50 Starter plan is gone. 10,000 free credits every month, then $1.50 per 1,000. Pro is $500 a month.
  • Exa - monitors fell from $15 to $2 per 1,000 requests. Search starts at $4 per 1,000.
  • QuillBot - unchanged. Free still means 125 words per paraphrase.

Why credits make budgets harder than seats

A seat price is predictable. You know how many people are on the team, so you know the bill. Credits are not like that, because the same task can cost one unit or twenty depending on what is inside it.

Two practical rules come out of that. First, measure a real working week before you choose an annual plan, not a demo. Second, pick the plan by the credit burn of your heaviest week, not the average one, because the overage charge lands on exactly the month you cannot afford it.

This is also why annual pricing is dangerous on a tool you have not run yet. The 20% you save on the yearly price disappears the first time you upgrade a tier because the monthly credits ran out in eleven days.

What to check before you start paying

  • The free limit in a unit, not a feeling. "Limited messages" is not a number. Find the credits or requests figure.
  • What counts as one unit. A page is not a page when it has tables. A message is not a message when it includes a long document.
  • The cheapest paid tier and the price at the cap. The gap between the entry plan and the next one is where most small teams get caught.
  • Whether the cap resets monthly or per session. A five-hour session cap and a monthly credit pool feel identical until the third week.

Do the arithmetic before the subscription

Three tools at $20 a month is $720 a year, and that is before anyone in the team asks for the $150 chatbot. The free AI cost calculator adds up a stack in dollars and shows the monthly total next to the annual one, so the decision is made on the yearly number rather than the monthly line.

For the per-tool detail, every tool page in the directory lists the monthly price, the free-plan limit and the point where the plan stops being enough. Nothing there is a press release.

Verdict: if you are choosing today, pick tools whose free plan is still a real plan, and read the credit unit before you pick the plan. The two tools here that removed their free tier did not announce it loudly. They just changed the page.

Questions

Questions people ask

Did every AI tool cut its free plan in 2026?

No. Chatbase and Ideogram removed theirs, LlamaIndex removed a mid-tier plan, Exa cut prices, and QuillBot still gives 125 words free.

What is the cheapest way to keep using Chatbase?

Basic at $150 a month billed monthly, or $120 a month billed yearly, with 4,000 message credits. There is no free plan left.

How many pages do 10,000 LlamaIndex credits cover?

About 10,000 plain pages in a month. A page full of tables can cost several credits each.

Why do AI tools bill in credits instead of seats?

Because usage varies per customer. Credits charge heavy users more without raising the price for everyone.
