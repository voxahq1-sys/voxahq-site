Qantas cuts 850 jobs and hands the work to Accenture in India

Home/News/Qantas job cuts

By the VOXA team · Qantas announcement of 10 November 2026

10 November 20267 min read

The short answer

Qantas will cut about 850 corporate jobs in Australia and hand the work to Accenture, with most of those roles moving to teams in India, as it rebuilds its back-end systems around automation and AI. The first round lands by mid-2027 and hits finance, procurement, human resources and marketing. That is roughly 3 per cent of the airline's 28,000 Australian workforce. Chief executive Vanessa Hudson told staff in an internal email that "this is hard news for some and I'm sorry for the uncertainty it brings".

What Qantas actually announced

The airline announced a multi-year strategic partnership with Accenture to "modernise its back-end systems, processes and data" as it steps up investment in technology and AI. Some corporate services move to Accenture, delivered by teams "mostly" in India and "some in Australia", and the company says those services will be "further transformed through automation and AI over time".

Read that sentence twice, because it is the whole story. The jobs are not being replaced by AI this year. They are being moved to a vendor whose contract says the work will be automated later. The headcount leaves Qantas either way; the technology arrives on Accenture's timetable.

The numbers

  • 850 Australian jobs cut
  • 3 per cent of the 28,000 Australian workforce
  • Mid-2027 for the first round
  • Functions affected first: finance, procurement, HR and marketing

Those four functions have something in common: they are process-heavy, documented, and measurable. That is exactly the profile that outsourcing firms have been targeting for twenty years, and it is also the profile that AI tooling handles best today. The airline did not need to say "AI replaced these jobs" for the direction to be obvious.

Why the framing matters

"Adapting to increased use of artificial intelligence" is doing a lot of work in Qantas's statement. It lets the company describe a cost decision as a technology decision, and it lets the vendor sell automation on top of the same contract. The union response was blunt: the Australian Services Union accused Qantas of going back on its word about offshoring jobs.

For the 850 people, the sequence is what matters. They are being told about a change that takes effect in 2027, in a labour market where the next employer will read "finance, procurement, HR or marketing" and see a role that is being automated everywhere. The notice period is long; the destination is narrow.

The pattern across Australian business

This is the second Australian story this week with AI at the centre. The same government that announced a new AI safety regulator on Tuesday is watching its flag carrier cut 850 head office roles and move the work offshore in the name of AI-driven modernisation. That is not a contradiction, but it is awkward: the regulator being created covers frontier model developers, not the companies reorganising around their output.

It is also the same pattern visible in the Canva cuts reported earlier this month and in the layoff announcements coming out of US healthcare AI companies. The wave is not research labs shedding staff. It is ordinary back-office work being reclassified as vendor work.

What it means if this reaches your company

Three things to watch for in your own organisation, because the Qantas playbook is now public.

First, the vendor announcement arrives before the automation. The cost saving is counted in year one, the automation arrives in year three, and the headcount never comes back. If your company announces an outsourcing deal "with an AI roadmap", the roadmap is the part that removes the remaining roles.

Second, the functions listed are the tell. Finance, procurement, HR and marketing are the four areas where documentation and process make handover easy. If your team's work is fully written down, it is portable - to a vendor, or to a model.

Third, the notice period is a benefit, not a guarantee. Qantas gave roughly 18 months of warning. That is unusually generous by the standards of the last two years of AI layoffs, and it exists because the work is going to a vendor that has to be staffed, not because the airline wanted to be kind.

What the airline gets

Qantas also used the day to flag fare increases and flight cuts on higher fuel costs. The outsourcing deal sits alongside that: a cost base that shrinks in the back office while the front of the business raises prices. For a company managing legacy systems and thin margins, moving 850 roles to a vendor is the least visible way to take cost out, because customers never see the change.

What customers will see, over time, is the automation the vendor sells. Fewer humans in procurement and finance does not show up on a boarding pass. It shows up when a refund takes four days instead of two, or when a schedule change lands without a phone number to call.

The question the announcement avoids

Nobody at Qantas has said how many of the 850 roles the vendor will need. An outsourcing deal of this shape usually keeps the work at similar volume for the first contract term, because someone still has to run the process while the systems are replaced. The saving is in the salary difference, not in the work disappearing. That is why the AI framing is doing so much lifting: it makes a labour arbitrage decision sound like a technology strategy, and it sets the expectation that the same work will need fewer people next time.

Questions people ask

How many jobs is Qantas cutting?

About 850 Australian corporate roles, roughly 3 per cent of its 28,000 Australian workforce.

Where are those jobs going?

To Accenture, with most of the work delivered by teams based in India and some in Australia, according to Qantas.

When do the cuts happen?

The first round is expected by mid-2027, with the rest over the following years.

Is AI doing the work now?

Not yet. Qantas describes the services as being "further transformed through automation and AI over time". The roles are moving to a vendor first.

Which teams are affected first?

Finance, procurement, human resources and marketing.
