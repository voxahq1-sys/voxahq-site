$250,000 to reserve a car that ships in 2028, and nobody has announced the price

Home/News/Tesla Roadster reservation

By the VOXA team · prices from Tesla’s own reservation page

20 October 20266 min readNumbers checked by hand

The short version

Tesla is taking deposits now for a car that enters production in 2028 without a published price. Performance costs $50,000 to reserve: $5,000 today and $45,000 within ten days. Thrust costs $250,000, split $5,000 and $245,000. The Rocket trim is invite only.

What was announced

Tesla unveiled the second-generation Roadster at an event near Waco, Texas, on 15 October 2026. The car uses ten cold-gas thrusters and high-pressure air tanks. In the stage demo it drove up a ramp, performed a barrel roll in the air and landed on its wheels.

Tesla’s own post claims 0-60 mph in 0.81 seconds. The company lists 300 miles of range and a top speed above 230 mph across all three trims. Production is scheduled for 2028.

The first $5,000 is fully refundable. The second payment is not: Tesla’s terms say the reservation can be cancelled if the balance is not completed within ten days. In practice, the deposit is a $50,000 or $250,000 commitment to a car with no published price.

The number nobody has

Tesla has not announced what the car will cost. Analysts quoted by the Los Angeles Times expect more than $250,000, which would make the Thrust reservation alone equal to the entire purchase price of most luxury cars.

That gap, between a deposit and a price, is the part worth watching. A reservation queue tells Tesla how much demand exists at a given deposit level. It does not tell a buyer what they will pay in 2028.

Why 2028 matters

Musk first promised the thrusters package in 2018. The reveal was delayed in 2020, 2021, 2023 and 2024, and slipped again in early October 2026 because of weather. A two-year wait is the optimistic reading of Tesla’s own history on this car.

Money held for two years has a cost. At a 4% annual return, $250,000 held from today to a 2028 delivery gives up roughly $20,000 in interest, on top of the depreciation risk of buying a first-generation vehicle.

The claim that matters more than the car

On 17 October, Musk said buyers will be able to ask the Roadster to perform the demo tricks autonomously. That single line repositions the product: the value is the autonomy software, not the thruster hardware.

It is the same pattern we track across AI tools, where the capability is promised first and the price is defined later. The guardrail advice in our AI agent failures guide applies here too: get the commitment in writing before you pay.

If you are actually considering a deposit

Three things to check before paying: the refund terms in writing, whether the price is locked at reservation, and what happens to your position if production slips again. Tesla’s page answers the first question partly and the other two not at all.

Questions people ask

Is the Tesla Roadster deposit refundable?

The first $5,000 is listed as fully refundable. The second payment is subject to cancellation if it is not completed within ten days, so read the terms before paying the balance.

How much will the Roadster cost?

Tesla has not said. Analysts quoted by the LA Times expect more than $250,000.

When can you buy one?

Production is scheduled for 2028. The car has been delayed repeatedly since it was first promised in 2017.
