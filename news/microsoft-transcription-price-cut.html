Microsoft cut cloud transcription to 10 cents an hour, and the catch is in the small print

Home/News/Microsoft transcription pricing

By the VOXA team · prices from Microsoft’s own model pages

20 October 20266 min readNumbers checked by hand

The short version

Microsoft now sells two versions of the same speech model. Batch transcription costs $0.10 per hour of audio. The streaming version costs $0.54, which is 5.4 times more for half a point of worse accuracy. Both prices are introductory and expire on 31 December 2026.

What Microsoft changed

On 1 October 2026, Microsoft AI released two speech-to-text models that share the same architecture but not the same bill. MAI-Transcribe-2 processes a finished audio file and returns a transcript. MAI-Transcribe-2-Streaming listens to live audio and returns partial text while the person is still talking.

The batch model scores 2.0% word error rate. The streaming model claims 2.5% and finalises each utterance about 0.13 seconds after the speaker stops. Both cover 60 languages with automatic language detection.

At $0.10 an hour, a thousand hours of audio costs $100. At $0.54, the same thousand hours costs $540, for the same words with slightly more errors. The only thing the premium buys is time: the transcript arrives while the call is happening, not after.

Microsoft is not the cheapest, despite the headline. It is the most accurate on the streaming leaderboard, and that accuracy is what you are buying at $0.54.

Three catches

One. Both rates are introductory and end on 31 December 2026. No replacement price has been published, so any budget built on $0.10 has a hole in it after January.

Two. Streaming costs 5.4 times the batch rate. That is the opposite of how volume pricing usually works, and it means the decision is about latency, not about cost per minute.

Three. This is a public preview with no service level agreement. For a call centre routing live calls, an SLA is not optional.

What to do if you already transcribe

If you pay a per-minute vendor, run the maths on your last month. A team processing 200 hours a month was likely paying between $1,200 and $2,400 at older rates. On batch pricing that becomes $20. The saving is real, and it is large enough to justify a migration test.

If you process a few hours a month, nothing changes. Local Whisper is still free per hour, and the setup cost is a laptop you already own. The full comparison, including the time cost, is in our guide to local Whisper versus cloud transcription.

If you need live text during a call, the question is not price. It is whether a 2.5% error rate is acceptable for what happens downstream: routing, summarising, or writing to a customer record.

Questions people ask

Is $0.10 per hour permanent?

No. Microsoft lists it as a limited-time price through 31 December 2026, with no standard rate published yet.

Why is streaming more expensive?

Streaming holds a connection open and returns partial results continuously, which uses more compute per minute of audio than processing a finished file once.

Should I switch from Whisper?

Only if you need speed or managed infrastructure. For batch work on a machine you own, local Whisper still costs nothing per hour.
