How to find every AI subscription you are paying for, and cut the ones you forgot

Home/Guides/AI subscription audit

By the VOXA team · Written from the billing pages of the major AI tools

29 October 20267 min readUpdated October 2026

The short answer

Most people are paying for three or four AI tools and actively using one. The leak is not the price of any single plan, it is the seat you bought once for a project, the annual renewal you forgot, and the overlapping tool that does the same job. This guide is a 30 minute audit: find every charge, match each one to a job you actually do, and cancel or downgrade the rest.

Step 1: List the charges before you judge them

Do not start from the tools you remember. Start from the money. Open your card statement or bank app, search for the last 90 days, and write down every charge that looks like software: everything billed monthly, and anything billed once a year.

Three places hide the ones people miss. The app store subscription list on your phone, which bills through Apple or Google and never shows up as a recognisable merchant. The workspace billing page inside a tool you share with a team, where you may be paying for seats you added months ago. And the annual renewals, which land as one large charge and are easy to forget between them.

Write each one down with three columns: what it is, what it costs per month, and what you last used it for. If you cannot fill the third column from memory, that is already your answer.

Step 2: Group by job, not by tool

AI tools are sold by brand but bought by job. The jobs that repeat for most people are: writing and editing, research and summarising, images, voice and video, transcription, coding, and automation. Put every charge under one of those headings.

Then apply one rule: one paid tool per job. If two tools sit under the same heading and you cannot say which one you opened last week, one of them is a subscription you are donating to. This is where most of the savings are, because the overlap is invisible on a statement.

Step 3: Check for the subscription and API overlap

This one is specific to AI and it is the most common quiet waste. A chat subscription and an API key are two different bills for the same model. If you pay for a chat plan and also run automations through an API, you are paying twice, and the API side is metered, so it grows without a fixed number on your statement.

Open the API billing page for each provider you have a key with and look at the last month's usage. If a workflow costs more than the chat plan and you barely use the chat plan, cancel the chat plan. If the API bill is tiny and the chat plan is your daily driver, keep the plan and treat the API as a small experiment budget with a hard cap.

Step 4: Do the annual versus monthly maths honestly

Annual plans are usually two months cheaper, which is a real discount if you will still be using the tool in twelve months. The trap is buying annual to save 17 percent on a tool you were about to drop. Rule of thumb: monthly for anything under three months of use, annual only for a tool you have already paid for monthly for two months in a row without thinking about cancelling.

When you do keep an annual plan, put the renewal date in your calendar now, with a reminder two weeks before. The reminder is the whole point. A renewal you see coming is a decision. A renewal you do not see is a charge.

Step 5: Downgrade before you cancel

Before cancelling anything, check the free tier. Several tools keep a usable free plan and only paywall the volume. If your use is occasional, free is the correct plan, and the only thing you lose is the convenience of not thinking about limits.

Also check seats. A team plan with three seats where two people left is a monthly gift. Removing seats is usually a two click change and it does not affect the person still using it.

What a clean setup looks like

One paid chat tool you use daily. One paid tool for the job that actually earns you money, whether that is images, code or voice. One metered API key with a spending cap for automation. Everything else on a free tier, with a calendar reminder on the two annual renewals you chose to keep. That is four line items, and every one of them has a job attached.

Run the same audit every quarter. Tools change their plans constantly, and a plan that was good value in January is often the one you cancel in April. If you want the news side of this, the news hub tracks price and plan changes as they happen, and our note on what subscriptions actually cost covers the smaller tools.

One last thing: cancel in the tool's own billing page, not through your bank. A card block looks like a failed payment, some tools suspend the account instead of closing it, and you can end up with a locked account and a bill you thought was gone.

Questions people are asking

How many AI subscriptions does the average person actually use?

Most people we hear from pay for three or four and use one or two weekly. The gap between paid and used is where the savings are.

Can I share one AI subscription with family or a team?

Only if the plan allows it. Most consumer plans are single user, and sharing usually breaks the terms. Team plans cost more per seat but are the legitimate route.

Is annual billing worth it for AI tools?

Only for a tool you have already paid monthly for two months without considering cancelling. Annual on a tool you are about to drop is a loss, not a discount.

What is the difference between a chat plan and an API key?

A chat plan is a fixed monthly price for the app. An API key bills per use, so the cost moves with your automations. Paying for both for the same model is usually waste.

How often should I repeat this audit?

Every quarter, plus two weeks before any annual renewal. Plans and prices change often enough that a yearly check misses things.
