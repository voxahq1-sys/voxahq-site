Best AI tools for writing product descriptions in 2026

Quick answer

For most stores, ChatGPT Plus at $20 a month writes product descriptions well enough, and Shopify Magic is already included if you sell on Shopify. Pay for Jasper only when several people write for one brand.

  • Cheapest per description: Rytr Lite at $9/mo
  • Best for one consistent brand voice: Jasper Pro at $59 per seat
  • No extra cost at all: Shopify Magic inside your Shopify plan

What a product description actually needs

A product description has one job: answer the three questions a buyer has before they add to cart. What is this, will it fit my case, and what will annoy me later. A generic AI model gets the first one right and guesses at the other two, which is why raw output reads like every other store.

The fix is not a better model. It is feeding the model the spec sheet: dimensions, materials, what is in the box, who it is not for. Every tool below gets noticeably better the moment you paste that in, and no tool fixes a missing spec sheet.

ChatGPT Plus, $20 a month

The default pick for a small store. You paste a product's specs, ask for three versions at different lengths, and pick one. It handles tone switches well, so a hardware store and a skincare brand can both use it.

Its limit is memory across a catalog. On a 200-product store you will be re-pasting the brand rules every session unless you keep a saved prompt. That is a workflow problem, not a model problem, and it is free to fix.

Claude Pro, $20 a month

Better than ChatGPT at holding a long rule sheet: banned words, sentence length, how to describe materials, what claims to avoid. If your descriptions have to pass a legal or brand review, this is the one that keeps the rules in place for the whole run.

The trade-off is usage limits on heavy days. If you are generating 500 descriptions in one sitting, you will hit a cap and wait.

Rytr, $9 to $49 a month

The cheapest way to produce volume. Lite is $9 a month, Personal is $19, and Pro is $49, all from Rytr's pricing page. For a store with 50 simple products and no brand voice to protect, the $9 tier does the job.

What you give up is judgement. Rytr produces clean sentences and little specificity, so every description needs a human pass to add the detail that makes it sell. At 50 products that is an afternoon; at 500 it is a week.

Copy.ai, $29 a month

Chat with several models behind one subscription, which sounds like a bargain until you want automation. Copy.ai's workflow product is priced for teams and starts in four figures a month, so for a small store it is effectively a $29 chat tool.

Jasper Pro, $59 per seat a month

Built for the case where several people write for one brand. You define the voice once, and every seat inherits it. If you are a solo store, you are paying $59 for a feature you cannot use.

Shopify Magic, included in your plan

If you sell on Shopify, this is already paid for. It writes a first draft inside the product editor using the product title and a few fields. The output is plain but usable, and the price is zero extra.

Its limit is scope. There is no bulk run across the catalog, and it will not learn your brand voice from previous products.

The cheapest setup that actually works

One $20 plan plus a saved prompt is enough for most stores under 200 products. Write the prompt once: audience, banned claims, sentence length, the three questions above, and one good example description. Then paste each spec sheet and ask for two variants.

Only move up to Jasper when a second writer joins, and only move down to Rytr when the catalog is simple enough that generic copy is acceptable.

What we did not test
