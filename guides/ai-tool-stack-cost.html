AI tool stack cost: what $0, $50 and $200 a month buys

Home/Guides/AI tool stack cost

By the VOXA team · we check every price by hand before it goes up

Updated October 6, 20266 min readPrices checked Oct 2026

Quick answer

A solo operator can run a complete AI stack for about $45 to $55 a month. The $200 tier exists for teams, not for better tools.

  • The free stack covers writing, recordings, 1,000 automation operations and daily video credits.
  • The $50 stack: one $20 assistant, a
    0 to
    6 notetaker, $9 automation, $5 to $8 video.
  • The $200 stack adds a second assistant, per-seat writing tools and business tiers.
  • Most budgets leak on a second assistant, per-seat tools bought for one person, and annual plans on credit-based tools.

AI subscriptions are cheap one at a time and expensive together. The question isn't which tool is best, it's which four tools actually earn their place this month.

Here's what each budget gets you, using the prices we checked in October 2026. And at the end, the four places your money usually leaks.

If none of that describes you, this stack is $200 of features you'll never open.

Where the money leaks

Two assistants, one habit. Most people use one every day and the other twice a month. Cancel the second until you notice it's gone.

Per-seat tools bought for one person. A $59 seat is fine for a team of writers and silly for a solo blog.

Annual billing on credit plans. Paying a year upfront for credits that expire monthly is the worst of both.

Enterprise tiers for compliance you don't need. Real value for regulated teams, pure cost for everyone else.

Three rules that cut the bill

  1. One tool per job. Two tools in one slot means one is wasted.
  2. Pay monthly until you've used it for 60 days, then take the annual discount.
  3. Review it every quarter. Prices and included models change constantly.

Choosing inside a category is its own job. Start with writing tools, meeting notetakers, or automation platforms.

Frequently asked questions

How much should a small business spend on AI tools?

A solo operator can cover everything useful for $45-55 a month: one assistant, one notetaker, one automation platform and a small video budget. Teams pay per seat from there. Most small businesses don't need to pass $200 until there's a specific volume problem.

Can I really work on free tiers alone?

For a while, yes. You get writing, recordings, 1,000 automation operations and daily video credits. What pushes you to pay is usually message caps and the two-scenario limit on free automation.

Is an annual plan worth it?

Only after 60 days of real use. Annual billing usually saves 15-20%, which is worth taking once a tool is part of your routine, and a trap for credit-based tools.

What should I cancel first?

The second assistant. It's the most duplicated spend and the least missed. Then any per-seat tool bought for one person, then any credit plan you haven't touched in two months.

Do these prices stay the same?

No. Every figure here was checked in October 2026, and AI pricing changes faster than almost anything else in software. Use this as a framework and check the current price before you buy.
