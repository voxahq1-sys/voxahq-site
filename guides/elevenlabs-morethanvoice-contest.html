ElevenLabs Year of More contest: how to enter, the rules, and the dates

By Raghav Walia
Home/Guides/Year of More contest

The short answer

Make a video in ElevenCreative between 30 seconds and 5 minutes, edited from more than one shot, put the competition watermark on it, post it on at least two platforms with #morethanvoice and #ElevenCreativeCompetitionVol2, tag @elevenlabs and @elevencreative, then submit the entry form with your links. The form is what counts - a post without a form is not an entry. Entries close 30 November 2026, 11:59pm ET.

ElevenLabs is running a $200,000 competition alongside its Black Friday sale, and most of the entry guidance floating around is out of date. I read the official brief on the entry page rather than the affiliate email, because the two disagree on the hashtag, the number of entries and the announcement dates. Everything below is from the brief itself, checked on 16 November 2026.

What the competition actually is

"The Year of More" is presented by ElevenCreative, the part of ElevenLabs that handles audio and video production rather than plain text to speech. The brief is open: show what you create with the toolkit, and show it as a finished video rather than a demo. It is free to enter, you can go solo or as a team, and there is no cap on how many entries you submit.

The four steps, in order

1. Make the piece

A finished video in ElevenCreative, 30 seconds to 5 minutes, and - this is the part people miss - edited from multiple shots. One long generation is not an entry. Think in cuts: eight to twelve shots of two or three seconds each reads as a film, while one continuous take reads as a demo.

2. Watermark it and post it

Add the competition watermark from the entry page, then post publicly on at least two of X, LinkedIn, YouTube, Instagram or TikTok. Your caption needs both hashtags - #morethanvoice and #ElevenCreativeCompetitionVol2 - and both tags, @elevenlabs and @elevencreative. Private posts and stories that disappear do not count.

3. Submit the form

The entry form asks for your post links and, more importantly, which ElevenCreative features you used and how. This is where judging is won: the brief says they are looking at depth of use, not the number of features ticked. If you used voice, music and sound effects to do a specific job, say what that job was.

4. Wait for the rounds

Winners are picked daily and announced in three batches - 23 November, 30 November and 3 December - with the Grand Prize on 10 December.

Read those three together and the strategy writes itself: a 40-second piece where music, voice and sound effects each do a job beats a 4-minute tour of every button.

The thing nobody tells you before you start

ElevenCreative runs on credits, and video generation is the expensive part. On the free plan I ran out before finishing a single multi-shot piece, which is why the competition is worth entering with a paid tier rather than scraping the free allowance - the entry needs a finished video, not a test render.

If you are going to buy credits anyway, do it during the sale: annual plans are up to 55% off until 1 December, and Creator is the first tier that includes the commercial licence, which matters if the piece is going to be published or broadcast.

A 50-second structure that fits the brief

If you want a starting point rather than a blank page, this is the shape that satisfies "multiple shots" without needing a week of editing:

  • 0-5s: one dark shot, one line of narration, no music yet
  • 5-20s: three or four shots that escalate, music enters at the third
  • 20-35s: the turn - the piece changes scale, sound effects carry the cut
  • 35-50s: pull back, one line of narration, music cuts to silence, title card

Keep the narration under 130 words for that runtime. Any more and the voice tramples the music.

What to write in the entry form

The form is the only part of the entry a judge reads closely, and most people fill it in badly - a list of tool names with no explanation. The brief asks which features you used and how, so write it as three short sentences, one per feature, each ending in a result.

An example that would score, written about a piece like the structure above:

  • Music: built a single bed that rises at the third shot, so the cut to the city scene lands on the beat instead of on a hard edit.
  • Sound effects: used a room hum under the opening line and a door whoosh on the transition, so the silence before the title card reads as a stop rather than a gap.
  • Voice: one narrator throughout, slowed on the last two lines so the pull-back shot has room to breathe.

Notice what those sentences have in common: each names a decision and the effect it had. That is what "depth of use" means. "Used music, SFX and TTS" scores nothing.

The four mistakes that void an entry

  1. Posting without the form. A public post with both hashtags is not an entry until the form is submitted with the links.
  2. Using an old hashtag. Earlier versions of this campaign asked for #contest. The current brief wants #morethanvoice and #ElevenCreativeCompetitionVol2.
  3. One continuous take. The brief explicitly asks for a piece edited from multiple shots.
  4. Posting on one platform. Two is the minimum, and the post has to stay public.

If you are doing this for the first time, the safest order is: make the piece, export it, post it on two platforms, then fill the form the same hour while the links are in front of you. Nothing about the entry is hard except remembering step three.

Questions people ask

What is the ElevenLabs Year of More contest?

A $200,000 competition run by ElevenCreative. You make a video between 30 seconds and 5 minutes in ElevenCreative, post it on two or more platforms with the required hashtags, and submit the entry form. Entries close 30 November 2026 at 11:59pm ET.

What hashtags do I need?

Both #morethanvoice and #ElevenCreativeCompetitionVol2, plus the tags @elevenlabs and @elevencreative. Many older write-ups still say #contest, which is not what the current brief asks for.

Can I enter for free?

The competition is free to enter, but you need ElevenCreative credits to make the video, and video generation uses them quickly. A finished multi-shot piece usually needs a paid credit balance rather than the free monthly allowance.
