Here's the thing about AI video: the headline price means almost nothing. What matters is how many seconds of finished video a credit buys you. Most tools hide that number, and it's the only number that decides whether your $12 plan is a bargain or a waste.

Short version: Google's Veo is the best value for generating clips from a prompt. Runway is for people who edit. OpusClip is for turning one long recording into ten short ones.

Do this one calculation before you subscribe

Runway's Standard plan sounds generous: 625 credits a month. Then you read that its flagship model charges 12 credits per second. That's about 52 seconds of video for $12.

Now compare the tool advertising 144,000 credits a year elsewhere. Sounds enormous. Whether it is depends entirely on what one credit buys.

So ask four questions before you pay:

  • What does one second of my chosen model cost?
  • Do unused credits expire each month?
  • Is there a watermark on my tier?
  • Can I use the output commercially?

If the answers aren't on the pricing page, that's your answer.

For product demos

Two approaches work, and they're not the same job.

Generated scenes. Veo through Google's plans is the most reliable at turning a prompt into usable footage, and the $19.99 tier removes the watermark, which matters the moment a clip goes anywhere public.

Your actual product. Start from a photo and animate it, or build the video from stills with a tool like Pictory. One warning: showing AI-generated footage of a product you haven't made yet is a brand risk, not a shortcut.

For ad creative at volume

If you need twenty versions of one ad, generating twenty videos is the wrong approach. Record once, then cut it with a clipping tool. Spend generation credits on the opening shot only.

That's the split most teams land on: one recording, ten clips, one or two generated scenes to open them.

How to keep the costs sane

  1. Write the shot list as text first. It's free and it saves the most money.
  2. Generate at low resolution until the shot works.
  3. Re-render only the shot you kept, at full quality.
  4. Cut and add captions in a normal editor. Captions are what make a clip watchable.
  5. Leave room in the budget for the shot that fails. It always happens.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use AI video in real ads?

Yes, though some platforms ask for disclosure and a few categories ban realistic depictions of people or events. Most brands keep generated footage to backgrounds and hooks rather than pretending a generated product shot is real footage.

How much video can I actually make for $12?

On Runway's Standard plan, the flagship model costs 12 credits per second against 625 credits a month, so roughly 50 seconds. Cheaper models in the same plan stretch much further. Check the per-second cost for the model you plan to use.

What about vertical videos for social?

For cutting long recordings into vertical clips, OpusClip at $15 a month does exactly that. For generating new vertical footage from a prompt, Veo, Kling and Pika all support it on plans from a few dollars.

Is the free tier enough to test?

Yes for testing. Free tiers limit clip length, add watermarks and often restrict commercial use. Google gives about 50 free credits a day, which is a fair way to judge quality before paying.

Where do voiceovers come from?

Usually a separate tool. Most video generators now add music or sound effects, but narration is its own job. Our guide to meeting notes and transcription tools covers the audio side.
